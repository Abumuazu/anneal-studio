<Brief msg="The usual answer meant holding other people's money.">
  Buyers wanted to offer a price instead of paying the sticker one. Sellers wanted to accept,
  decline or counter, and know an accepted offer would actually pay. The usual answer is a custom
  escrow that holds the buyer's money until the deal closes. Here, building one would have meant
  taking on the compliance that comes with holding other people's money.
</Brief>

<Questions
  msg="Who holds the money? The bank does."
  heading="Three questions we answered before drawing a screen."
>
  <Question title="Who holds the money while the seller decides?">
    The buyer's own bank, same as any card payment. The card is authorised when the offer is made,
    and the funds stay there until the seller accepts.
  </Question>
  <Question title="Do we need to hold anyone's money at all?">
    A custom escrow would have put the client in the business of holding funds. We recommended a
    card hold through Stripe Checkout instead, so the money stays on the buyer's card until the
    seller accepts and we never hold it. How a marketplace is regulated depends on where it
    operates, so the client's advisers confirm that side.
  </Question>
  <Question title="What happens if the seller says nothing?">
    Every offer has an expiry. Card holds usually last about seven days, so offers run out inside
    that window. When one does, the hold is released and the buyer is free to try again.
  </Question>
</Questions>

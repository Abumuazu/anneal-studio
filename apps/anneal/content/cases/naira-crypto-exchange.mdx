<Brief msg="Two sets of requirements before any design.">
  The founders wanted people in Nigeria to buy and sell crypto against naira on their phone.
  Nigeria's Investments and Securities Act 2025 brings digital assets under securities regulation,
  so this is regulated activity. Apple adds its own requirements for exchange apps: licensing, a
  legal entity and supporting documents, which depend on how the business is set up and where the
  app is distributed. Miss either one and the app doesn't launch, however good it looks. This is how
  we approached the project, not legal advice, and each client confirms the requirements with its
  own advisers.
</Brief>

<Questions
  msg="Who holds the licence decides a lot."
  heading="Three questions we answered before drawing a screen."
>
  <Question title="Who holds the licence?">
    We mapped which parts of the flow sit with the licensed partner and which sit with the client,
    so the client could see where its own responsibilities start.
  </Question>
  <Question title="Which entity goes on the App Store listing?">
    Apple reviews exchange apps against the licences and legal entity behind the listing. We planned
    the publishing set-up around that from the start and flagged it for the client to confirm with
    its advisers.
  </Question>
  <Question title="What does the partner's API cover?">
    Plugging into a licensed provider's API doesn't automatically hand you their licence. We drew a
    clear line between what the API handles and what the business still has to own.
  </Question>
</Questions>

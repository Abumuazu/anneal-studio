<Brief msg="Two sets of requirements before any design.">
  The founders wanted customers to buy and sell crypto on their phones. Delivering that experience meant looking beyond the trade screen. Identity checks, provider responsibilities, publishing requirements and the path from a confirmed trade to a payout all affected the product.

We mapped those dependencies before designing the main flow, so the interface reflected how the service was intended to operate.

</Brief>

<Questions
  msg="Who holds the licence decides a lot."
  heading="Three questions we answered before drawing a screen."
>
  <Question title="Begin with who is responsible">
  The product connected to a partner's services. We separated the work performed through that integration from responsibilities that remained with the client. Access to an API was treated as a technical connection, rather than an assumption about the client's permissions.

Publishing requirements were considered alongside the product, including the intended entity and developer account behind the listing.

</Question>
  <Question title="Make the first trade understandable">
  Verification comes before trading. The flow explains the required BVN and identity checks and shows progress as the customer moves through them.

For a trade, the customer sees the quoted rate and the time available to confirm it. Rate confirmation, status and receipts help explain what is happening rather than leaving the customer to infer it from a changing balance.

</Question>
  <Question title="Connect the transaction to its outcome">
  The experience includes buying and selling, payouts to a verified bank account, trade history and individual receipts.
</Question>
</Questions>
